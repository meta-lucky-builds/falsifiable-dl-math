# Literature scout: objectives, information, probability

Scope: Fenchel-Young / generalized entropies, proper scoring rules, label-noise losses, OT losses, gradient estimators (Stein, control variates, forward-gradient), conformal / PAC-Bayes as training losses, neural collapse, IB / rate-distortion, heavy-tailed noise.
Tool: alphaXiv only (about 18 discovery calls, 20 papers fetched). No OpenReview or web search was done. Every ID below was returned by the tool. I did not open the full text of every paper (see the "Read depth" column), so the evidence grades are about what the paper reports, not what I re-derived.
Date context: today is 2026-11-04. Many IDs are 26xx; I could not independently check that any of them has been peer reviewed.

Evidence key: S = seeds reported, T = baseline tuned or taken from the authors' own prior tables, R = independent replication seen.
Read depth: FULL = whole text or full report read; REPORT = alphaXiv AI report only; ABS = abstract + intro + grep of results.

## 2. Tried and disappointing (negative, null or unreplicated)

1. **Fixed ETF classifier does not speed up training.** 2411.01248 states (in its intro, citing earlier work) that fixing the classifier to a canonical simplex ETF reduces parameters but does not improve convergence speed, since the backbone must still match the targets. Its fix (nearest ETF each step) is the only variant claiming a speedup; I did not check the magnitude.
2. **Learning the reference measure q in f-divergence losses gave no gain** over uniform q (2501.18537). Jensen-Shannon and squared Hellinger lost to KL. Pretraining gain from alpha=1.5 is 0.04 points of next-token accuracy.
3. **Structured proper-scoring losses did not beat CE** (2606.29471): ablations show no consistent benefit from the added graph, ridge or margin parts; noisy-label baselines and prior-adjustment methods won in the reported cells. Five seeds, small data, so this is weak but honest.
4. **Forward-gradient methods remain far behind backprop.** W-perp on MNIST-1D reaches 0.58 vs 1.00 train accuracy (2511.03110); Split-FG reaches ppl 387 vs 150 (2607.16612); the 2312.04709 authors say the gap is "large". Naive forward-gradient training of a transformer trunk was worse than leaving the trunk random and frozen (ppl 1733 vs 668). A "light trunk" that matches parameters to probe budget only ties the frozen control (671.6 vs 667.6). Memory savings cost 1.5-4.5x step time and checkpointing is stronger.
5. **Unbiasedness does not pay.** The unbiased preconditioned estimator (W~^P) had the lowest bias but lower accuracy than the biased low-variance one (2511.03110, one width, one run).
6. **Forward-Forward real-data ceiling** (2606.06539): the gap to backprop widens with class count and brings no practical memory advantage.
7. **RL-style Tsallis commitment has a sweet spot below q=1** and can collapse (GARL at HotPotQA / MuSiQue; q=1 worse than q=0.75), 2604.25907. Rao-Blackwellized RLOO alone did not escape cold start.
8. **Label smoothing calibration is inconsistent**: improves pre-temperature-scaling ECE with a small delta, but is worse than CE after temperature scaling (2402.03979).
9. **Harmonic-loss benefits are mostly small-model / interpretability**: +0.9 pt ImageNet, -0.013 nats GPT-2 in the authors' own implementation (2502.01628). I did not see a replication of the grokking claim.
10. **Proper scores are not interchangeable in finite training** (2608.28482), but that result is single-seed, so do not use it to rank them.
11. **Eps-softmax gain is partly the MAE term**; strong baselines are within a few points at high noise (2508.02387). Noise-robust loss tables in this literature are mostly produced by the same group against its own previous tables.

## 3. Open gaps (all TENTATIVE: absence of evidence in about 18 alphaXiv searches, not proof)

For each: what I searched, what I found, and a minimal Colab-sized test.

**G1. Entmax-1.5 / alpha-divergence FY loss under label noise.**
Searched: "Fenchel-Young Tsallis sparsemax label noise", "generalized cross entropy Tsallis robust". Found: generalized cross-entropy variants (2603.19874 snippet), ANTIDOTE (alpha-divergence reweighting, but not the FY loss), the Tsallis continuum for reasoning (2604.25907, theory only on noise). 2501.18537 lists label-noise robustness as future work in the report I read. I did not find the FY alpha=1.5 loss evaluated on CIFAR with symmetric and asymmetric noise next to GCE / eps-softmax. Test: CIFAR-10/100 ResNet-18, noise 0/20/40/60/80%, alpha in {1, 1.25, 1.5, 2}, 3 seeds, GCE and eps-softmax as baselines.

**G2. Harmonic loss is a softmax on log-distance logits, but nobody compares it to the obvious controls.**
Derivation (mine, from the formula in 2502.01628): p_i proportional to d_i^(-n) = exp(-n log d_i). Searched: "harmonic loss power-law softmax q-exponential", "harmonic loss replication". Found: 2603.10225 (adds distances only). I did not find a comparison of harmonic loss to CE with the same distance logits (-d_i or cosine with learned scale), nor to a Tsallis q>1 link. Test: modular addition grokking and CIFAR-10 with four heads: dot-product CE, -d^2 CE, -n log d CE (= harmonic), cosine CE with temperature. If -d^2 CE already removes grokking, the claim attributes the effect to the wrong ingredient.

**G3. Whether the reported alpha=1.5 gain survives the numerically correct CE baseline.**
Searched: "softmax cross entropy numerical", "softmax collapse". Found 2610.31642 and 2610.36427; neither compares against f-divergence or entmax losses. The loss comparison in 2501.18537 and every robust-loss table use the standard CE gradient p - y. Whether bf16 collapse (margin > 5.5) matters for a CE baseline trained to zero loss is unknown; the authors of 2610.31642 themselves say it may be rare at large scale. Sparse FY losses have exactly zero gradient beyond a margin, which is a different regime. Test: small LM or CIFAR in bf16, CE vs fixed CE vs entmax-1.5, log margin distribution and gradient sum-to-zero violation.

**G4. Heavier-than-Gaussian tails of the forward-gradient estimator vs sign/orthogonalized updates.**
Derivation (mine): forward gradient is (v^T g) v with Gaussian v, so each coordinate is a product of Gaussians (non-Gaussian tails). 2602.07425 proves sign/Muon robustness to heavy-tailed noise; 2511.03110 borrows only Muon's Newton-Schulz tool, not its update rule; 2607.16612 attributes the failure of naive forward gradients to "Adam updates every noisy coordinate too aggressively". Searched: "forward gradient heavy-tailed", "negative results forward-mode", "heavy-tailed noise optimizer". I found no paper that applies Lion/Muon-style sign or orthogonalized updates to forward-gradient estimates, or tests whether Split-FG's learning-rate multiplier rho is replaceable by sign/clipping. Test: Split-FG style head-exact / trunk-JVP on a small MLP or char-LM; compare Adam + rho vs Lion vs Muon vs clipped SGD; report trunk-update kurtosis.

**G5. Stein identity for bounded and heavy-tailed q-Gaussians is not connected to the sharpness-aware / zeroth-order setting it names.**
Searched: "q-Gaussian sharpness-aware minimization perturbation", "Student-t perturbation variational". Found: 2603.03673 (abstract says SAM could benefit, the results I could see only confirm bounded variance), 2607.25376 (Student-t likelihood for BNN, snippet only), SAM papers using Gaussian or l2-ball perturbations. The theorem covers q<1 (bounded); the heavy-tailed side (q>1) was not covered in what I read. Test: random-weight-perturbation SAM (no inner gradient) with Gaussian vs q-Gaussian (q=0.5) noise on a small CNN, compare estimator variance and test accuracy over 5 seeds.

**G6. Generalized-entropy output maps used in the loss but adaptive-temperature scaling only in attention.**
Searched: "entmax language model pretraining loss", "Tsallis attention". Found: ASEntmax (2506.16640) uses learned length-dependent scaling inside attention; 2501.18537 uses alpha=1.5 for the loss but with no learned scale. I found no paper that learns alpha (or an output temperature) per token / per position in the loss, or pairs a FY loss with entmax attention in one small LM. Test: 10-30M parameter LM, softmax/entmax in attention x CE/FY-1.5 in the loss (2x2), 3 seeds.

**G7. Conformal risk training with sparse (FY) outputs.**
Searched: "conformal prediction sparsemax entmax prediction set Fenchel-Young". Found: 2602.19498 ("Softmax is not enough for adaptive conformal", snippet only, possibly close), 2512.19142 (convex set-prediction loss, Lovasz extensions, snippet only). I did not find a CRT-style training loss (2510.08748) driven by a sparsemax / entmax output whose support is itself the prediction set. This gap may be partly filled by the two snippet-only papers, which I did not open. Test: CIFAR-100, entmax outputs, set size and coverage at fixed alpha vs softmax + APS.

**G8. Proper-score family (Brier, spherical, Beta) as plain supervised next-token or classification losses at several seeds.**
Searched: "Brier loss spherical score training deep classifiers language models at scale". Found only the RL-reward study (2608.28482, one seed) and 2606.29471 (small data). I found no multi-seed pretraining-style comparison of spherical or Beta-family scores. 2501.18537 covers f-divergences, which is a different family. Test: small LM or CIFAR-100 at 5 seeds with log, Brier, spherical, Beta(2,8), alpha=1.5 FY.
